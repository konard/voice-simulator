Run the 3D FDTD solver of the Rust engine on several threads, with a single-core fallback (issue 24). The two passes of the gather kernel take index ranges (`links_pass`, `cells_pass`), `fdtd/partition.rs` splits them into parts of equal work, and `fdtd/parallel.rs` holds a `Team` of a leader and helpers that meet at a spin barrier (the only `unsafe` code of the core crate). One thread keeps the scatter kernel of issue 23; a team runs only when the grid has at least `THREAD_MIN_LINKS` links. Natively, `voice-simulator-cli render-job <job> --threads <n|auto> [--thread-min-links <n>]` renders on `n` threads (default 1) with the same SHA-256, and `kernel-steps` times the kernel. `node scripts/build-wasm.mjs --threads` builds `src/engine/voice-simulator-threads.wasm` with the nightly compiler of `THREADS_TOOLCHAIN` (atomics, a shared imported memory of `THREAD_MEMORY` pages); `loadEngineModule(url, { threads })` takes 1 (the default), a positive integer or `'auto'`, loads that file with a pool of Web Workers or `worker_threads` (`createThreadPool`, `withTeam`), and falls back to one thread without the file, without `SharedArrayBuffer` or without cross-origin isolation. `engine.threads`, `engine.pool`, `engine.close()` and `renderer.threads` tell what a render got. Renders of `'Hello.'` at `high` in a 40 cm room on 2 and 4 threads equal those on one thread under `===` (`tests/rust-threads.test.js`). The heavy test `performance-threads` timed 2000 kernel steps on five grids on 1 to 4 threads, natively and in WebAssembly: no grid was 1.3 times faster on 2 threads than on one (at best 1.16×), so `THREAD_MIN_LINKS` is `usize::MAX` and a team starts only on request (`threadMinLinks`, `--thread-min-links`); the report is `docs/reports/performance-threads.md`. The example app sends `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy` and `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy` from its dev and preview servers and loads `coi-serviceworker` (MIT) only with `VITE_CROSS_ORIGIN_ISOLATION=1`; the published app stays on one thread.
