Add frequency-dependent losses to the 1D solver (issue 36). `src/acoustics/losses.js` gives the viscothermal boundary-layer loss of a duct (`viscothermalLoss(frequency, area, shape)`, which grows with √f, scaled by `LOSS_SHAPE`) and the bandwidths of Hawks & Miller (1995) (`publishedBandwidth(frequency, f0)`). The `Waveguide` filters its waves with first-order loss filters, one per group of sections (`LOSS_STAGES`), so its constant `lossFactor` keeps only the loss of a narrow constriction. Its walls now yield as a mass–resistance–spring per unit area, with new head parameters `wallMass`, `wallResistance` and `wallStiffness` (defaults 2.1 g/cm², 800 and 84 500 in cgs units). The lip radiation load adds a baffle between a free pipe end (0) and an infinite one (1): `configure(area, rate, baffle)` with the default `RADIATION_BAFFLE`. `tubeTransfer()` uses the same losses, walls and baffle, takes `walls: false` for rigid walls, and gains a `'pressure'` output at a probe. `impulseSpectrum()` and `resonances()` (the formant bandwidths of an impulse response) are exported. Across the 11 vowels of Story (2008), the mean error of the waveguide's F1–F3 bandwidths against Hawks & Miller drops from 34.7 % to 23.7 %. The waveguide keeps its nose in its own arrays, filtered with the same losses (`nasalLoss` times stronger) and scattered in one pass with the tract; a closed, silent nose is skipped, so the audit sentence renders within 4 % of the time it took before on tier `medium`. `NoseLine` and the 2D and 3D solvers keep the constant boundary loss (`boundaryLossFactor`). The vowel calibration tables of the waveguide and the 3D solver were re-fitted, so rendered audio changes on every tier.
