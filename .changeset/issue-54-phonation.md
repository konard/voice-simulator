Phonation types (issue 54). The phonation of a mode span (`[breathy]`, `[creaky]`, `[pressed]`, `[lax]`, `[falsetto]`, `[harsh]`, `<vs:phonation>`, the VoQS marks of IPA) is now heard: the plan gets `breath`, `creak`, `falsetto` and `harsh` channels (τ 0.03 s), set from the `PHONATION` table (breathy Rd 2.5 + breath 1, lax 1.8, pressed 0.4, creaky 0.5 + creak 1, falsetto 2.3 + falsetto 1 + breath 0.3, harsh 0.4 + harsh 1) by `phonationChannels()`; the Rd of the LF source is clamped at 0.3 instead of 0.5. The glottis adds breath noise, creaks an octave lower with double pulsing and jitter, raises falsetto an octave at -6 dB with a steeper source and makes harsh voice noisy and irregular. `a̤` and `a̰` make one vowel breathy or creaky (degree 0.67), the breathy-voiced stops of Hindi get a breathy voiced release of 80 ms, and the new head parameter `finalCreak` (default 0) creaks the low end of a statement. The fold source is driven by the same channels (adduction and fold tension). New exports: `PHONATION`, `PHONATION_CHANNELS`, `PHONATION_SOURCE`, `PHONATION_ANALYSIS`, `BREATHY_RELEASE`, `FINAL_CREAK`, `SEGMENT_DEGREE`, `MIN_F0`, `phonationChannels`, `voiceRd`, `finalCreakAmount`, `noiseBreathiness`, `phonationF0`, `cpp`, `jitterPercent`, `glottalPeriods`, `pulseF0`, `pulseH1H2`. The Rust engine realises the same source (interface version 4: frames carry the four channels). Text without markup gives the same samples as before, except the Hindi breathy-voiced stops. See docs/markup.md.
