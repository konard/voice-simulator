Change the quality tier in the middle of an utterance while streaming (issue 29). New exports: `AdaptiveStream` and `switchGrid` (src/engine/adaptive-stream.js), `StreamGovernor` (src/perf/lod.js) and `SOURCE_STATE` (the layout of the voice source state). `RenderOptions.startTime` starts a renderer in the middle of an utterance; `VoiceRenderer` and `RustVoiceRenderer` gain `saveSources()` and `restoreSources(state)`, and `Glottis` gains `saveState()` and `restoreState(state)`, so the new tier continues the pitch period, the random generator, the double pulsing of a creak and the falsetto low-pass of the old one (`SOURCE_STATE.LENGTH` is 12). `AdaptiveStream` renders every tier at one stream rate, starts the new tier 30 ms early so its tract fills, and crossfades over 25 ms with a fade that is linear for correlated signals and equal-power for unrelated ones, after scaling the new tier to the level of the old one. `StreamGovernor` goes down within 0.1 s when the load is above 0.8, at once on an underrun, and one tier up after 3 s of headroom, never back to a tier it left. The worker, with `auto: true`, streams at `streamRate` (default 48000 Hz), posts `{type: 'tier', id, at, from, level, reason, gainDb, solver, gpu, model}` for every switch, takes `{type: 'underrun', id}` from the player and adds `level` to `end`; it no longer restarts an utterance that is too slow (`overload` now only means the restart on the CPU after WebGPU failed) and no longer uses `OverloadDetector`. The Rust engine has the new exports `vs_renderer_seek`, `vs_renderer_sources_save` and `vs_renderer_sources_restore`. The example app sends the player's rate and its underruns, follows the `tier` messages at their time and shows the number of tier switches. Its `WorkletPlayer` in port mode now counts the samples of a chunk before it transfers them, so `time()` follows playback instead of staying at 0 (the transfer detached the buffer, and the count read a length of 0). In Chromium with the CPU throttled 6×, 22.8 s of speech switched from `medium` to `low` and then `minimal` while speaking, with no restart and no underrun after the first switch (`experiments/issue-29-app-switches.txt`). Measured with `experiments/issue-29-switch-levels.mjs`: `aː` from `low` to `minimal` is within 0.20 dB of the plain render in every 5 ms window, from `medium` to `high` within 1.12 dB; the 25 ms overlap of `sː` from `medium` to `low` is 0.46 dB above the 25 ms before it (0.54 dB without a switch).
