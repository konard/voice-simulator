Intonation models per language (issue 51). Every phrase gets a tune from its sentence type: English nuclear tones for statements, yes/no, wh-, echo, alternative and tag questions, lists, exclamations, commands, parentheses and calls; the Russian intonation constructions IK1 to IK7 with the centre on the stressed vowel; Hindi accent phrases; in Mandarin the tones stay and a question without a particle or question word is raised towards its end. Focus (`*word*`, `<emphasis>`, `ˈˈ`) moves the nucleus to the word and compresses what follows. The text front end writes the sentence type into the IPA (`↘ ?` wh-questions, `↗ ?` echo questions and asking tags, `ˈti ↗ | ˈɔɹ ˈkɑfi ↘ ?` for `Tea or coffee?`, `{IK4 … IK4}` for short Russian а-questions, `{IK5 … IK5}` for как-exclamations) and breaks stretches of more than 14 syllables with `|` before a conjunction; `|` now ends a phrase with a continuation rise (Russian IK3 at 0.7). Tunes can be named in IPA (`{IK3 … IK3}`), inline (`[tune yesno]…[/tune]`) and SSML (`<vs:tune name="echo">`). `estimatePitch` no longer halves the pitch of a level vowel whose period falls between two lags. New exports: `TUNES`, `TUNE_NAMES`, `TUNE_LABELS`, `FOCUS`, `INTONATION_RATE`, `analyzeIntonation`, `intonationLanguage`, `phraseTuneName`, `markPhrasing`, `PHRASE_CONJUNCTIONS`, `ALTERNATIVE_CONJUNCTIONS`, `ENGLISH_AUXILIARIES`, `MAX_PHRASE_SYLLABLES`, `MIN_PHRASE_SYLLABLES`, `englishAuxiliary`, `ZH_WH_WORDS`, `SUBHARMONIC_TOLERANCE`. The pitch of every sentence changes; see docs/markup.md.
