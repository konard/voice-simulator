Improve the quality of the voice (issue 9). Full 3D acoustics are the default of the library, the CLI, the server and the web app: the new `studio` tier (0.5 cm cells, `DEFAULT_AUDIO_TIER`) resolves the incisors, the piriform fossae, the vallecula and transverse modes above 5 kHz, and the `reference` tier (0.4 cm) goes further; `tier: 'medium'` (`--tier medium`) keeps the real-time 1D waveguide. The web app renders slow tiers ahead, shows the progress and starts playback once the rest of the utterance will be ready before the player reaches it. The 3D model gains its own piriform fossae and vallecula. Audio resumes after the tab was in the background, and the release before this one stays online at /v2/.

Female and child voices no longer sound like a high-pitched man. The anatomy of the mid-size female and child heads is fitted to the women's and children's vowel formants of Hillenbrand et al. (1995). A new `glottalChink` head parameter (mm²) models the posterior gap that most women leave open: it low-pass filters the glottal pulse and widens the formants as Hanson and Chuang (1999) measured. The female and child presets use 1 mm², which raises their H1*-A3* above the male preset by 10-11 dB, against 9.6 dB measured. The 3D solver gets its own vowel calibration table, and `spectralTilt` and `harmonicLevel` in the analysis module measure H1-H2 and H1-A3.

The head model can now be compared with real anatomy. New geometry modules read and write STL, slice meshes along any plane, find their open ends, voxelise them, trace the medial centreline from the glottis to the lips and measure the area function. They run on the model's own meshes and on the CC0 Dresden Vocal Tract Dataset (Birkholz et al. 2020). Six of that dataset's MRI vocal tracts ship as test fixtures, and `scripts/fetch-dvtd.mjs` fetches any other file of the 1.1 GB archive on demand through HTTP range requests.

The mouth is rebuilt after measured anatomy: the teeth lie on an arch fitted to the 17 Open-Full-Jaw scans, with their crown widths, the lips outline the acoustic opening, the jaw opens per phoneme as G-OBIM measured on MRI, and the palate and velum follow skull, CT and MRI norms. `fetchHra` reads the mouth and larynx of the HuBMAP Human Reference Atlas, and `mouthKeyPoints`, `modelKeyPoints` and `compareKeyPoints` cut any glTF mouth like MRI slices and compare ten key points automatically. Each part (teeth, tongue, palate, velum) can come from the procedural model, the atlas (`hra-male`, `hra-female`) or any glTF (`anatomySourceFromGlb`), fitted to the model's dental key points and decimated to the level of detail (the `anatomy` option, `npm run export:model -- --anatomy`).

Talking animation for character models: `visemeTrack` (CLI `visemes`, server `GET /api/visemes`) turns an utterance's articulation into ARKit blendshapes, VRM 1.0 expressions or Oculus visemes, frame by frame. `bindRig` binds the morph targets of any model, whatever convention they follow (ARKit and MetaHuman, Ready Player Me, Oculus and VRChat visemes, VRM 1.0 and 0.x, VRoid, MMD), `rigFrame` and `characterTrack` give their weights, and `vrmVowelBinds` reads the vowel expressions of a VRM file. The web app gets a Talking character panel that animates a procedural face or a `.vrm`, `.glb` or `.gltf` model the user loads.
