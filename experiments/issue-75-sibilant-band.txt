Findings (2026-11-24):
- Wideband, male: /s/ ("sea") spreads its noise over 4.5-7 kHz with the most
  energy at 6-7 kHz (centroid 4.2-4.8 kHz); /ʃ/ ("she") gathers it at
  5-6 kHz (centroid 5.0-5.3 kHz). Female: /s/ centroid 4.1-4.6 kHz, /ʃ/
  5.4-5.9 kHz. Natural /ʃ/ peaks near 2.5-3.5 kHz, well below /s/ (Stevens
  1998, ch. 8; Jongman, Wayland & Wong 2000, JASA 108: 1252), so the
  simulator's /ʃ/ is about 2.5 kHz too high and sits above /s/'s centroid.
- At telephone (band limit 3.4 kHz) both keep almost no in-band noise and
  look alike (centroids about 2.0 and 1.9 kHz), which fits the "sells" ->
  "shells" and "sea" -> "she" confusions of the round trip.
- Moving PLACES.postalveolar.location back (src/articulation/phones.js)
  lowers /ʃ/ only a little: 0.87 -> 5.0-5.3 kHz, 0.85 -> 4.9-5.1 kHz,
  0.83 -> 4.4-4.5 kHz (male, wideband). Reaching 3 kHz needs a longer front
  cavity (lip protrusion or a sublingual cavity) than the place alone gives.
- Any change to the sibilant noise changes the training audio: the HMM must
  then be retrained from a fresh corpus (56 files of 400 utterances, about
  20 min each on one core, plus about 12 min of training). Not done in this
  pull request; left as the next lead for the English telephone sound WER.
