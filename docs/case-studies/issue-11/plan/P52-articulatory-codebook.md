## Goal

`buildCodebook` makes a table: each row is one position of the tongue, lips
and velum, together with the sound that the simulator makes in that position
(formants and spectral envelope). For the envelope of one analysis frame the
table returns the 16 rows that sound most alike. The table is computed by the
simulator itself at first use, is the same on every machine, and is not
shipped in the package.

## Why

- Recognition here is the simulator run in reverse. The inversion ({{P53}})
  needs, for every frame, a short list of positions that could have made the
  sound. Many different positions make the same sound (Atal et al. 1978), so
  one answer per frame is wrong by design; a list is needed, and the choice is
  made later by continuity in time.
- A table made by the forward model needs no recordings and no training, so
  it is free of licences, and it covers every sound the simulator can speak.
- A search in a table costs far less than an optimisation per frame, which
  keeps the streaming path inside one core.

## Current state

- The forward path exists. `tractShape(state, head)` in
  `src/articulation/tract-shape.js` turns channel values into 44 areas
  (`TRACT_SECTIONS`) and a length. `tubeTransfer(areas, length, frequency)`,
  `tubeSpectrum` and `tubeFormants(areas, length, {count: 4})` in
  `src/acoustics/transfer.js` give the transfer function of that tube.
- `tubeTransfer` builds new complex objects for every section and frequency,
  and `tubeSpectrum` evaluates 1000 frequencies (step 5 Hz to 5000 Hz). This
  is fine for a few shapes and too slow for tens of thousands.
- `tubeTransfer` knows only blind side tubes (`branches`). It cannot compute a
  sound that leaves through the nose, so nasal consonants and nasal vowels
  have no analytic spectrum today; only the time-domain renderers make them.
- Phoneme targets exist: `vowelTarget` and `vowelChannels` (31 vowels) and
  `consonantTarget` (66 consonants) in `src/articulation/phones.js`.
- The default tract length is 16.5 cm (`HEAD_PARAMETERS.vocalTractLength`,
  range 11 to 20), while `REFERENCE_LENGTH` in `tract-shape.js` is 17.5 cm.
- No code goes from a sound back to a tract position.

## What to do

Create `src/recognition/codebook.js`.

1. Tract channels of the table, in this order, with the range used for
   scaling (exported as `CODEBOOK_CHANNELS`, a list of `{name, min, max}`):
   `height` 0..1, `backness` 0..1, `rounding` 0..1, `rhotic` 0..1,
   `palatalization` -1..1, `lipAmount` 0..1, `lipArea` 0..2 (cm²),
   `tongueAmount` 0..1, `tongueArea` 0..2 (cm²), `tongueLocation` 0..1,
   `tongueWidth` 0.02..0.08, `velum` 0..1. The other nine channels are about
   the source and are not part of the table.
2. Frequency axis. `mel(f) = 2595 * log10(1 + f / 700)`. `GRID` = 24
   frequencies for a tract of `L0` = 16.5 cm: 16 points equally spaced in mel
   from 150 to 2700 Hz, then 8 points equally spaced in mel from 2700 Hz
   (not repeated) to 5200 Hz. For a head of length `L` the point `GRID[i]` is
   observed at `GRID[i] * L0 / L` Hz (a tube that is longer by a factor has
   all resonances lower by that factor).
3. Fast transfer function `tractResponse(areas, length, frequencies, out)`
   in the same file: the same chain matrices, wall loss and lip radiation as
   `tubeTransfer`, written with separate real and imaginary numbers in local
   variables, no objects, result in dB (`20 * log10`) into `out`. A test
   compares it with `tubeTransfer`.
4. Features of one row from its areas (`tractShape` with the head of step 6):
   1. Evaluate `tractResponse` on a grid of 25 Hz from 100 to 5200 Hz.
   2. Formants: the first four local maxima; refine each with a parabola
      through the three points around it; bandwidth = the distance between
      the two frequencies where the curve is 3 dB below the peak (linear
      interpolation). A missing formant is stored as 0.
   3. Envelope: the value at each of the 24 `GRID` frequencies (linear
      interpolation), minus the mean of the first 16.
   4. Key: take the first 16 envelope values, subtract their least-squares
      straight line over the point index (this removes level and spectral
      tilt, which belong to the voice source), and take the cosine transform
      coefficients 1 to 6:
      `key[n] = sum over i of e[i] * cos(pi * n * (i + 0.5) / 16)`.
5. Rows with `velum >= 0.1` cannot use step 4. For them render 80 ms of a
   steady sound with `renderPlan` (`solver: 'waveguide'`, 16000 Hz,
   `seed: 1`). The plan is made by hand: `frameRate` 200, `frameCount` 17,
   `duration` 0.08, `segments` and `tokens` empty, and every channel a
   `Float32Array` filled with one value (the row value, or
   `CHANNEL_DEFAULTS` with `f0` 100 for the source channels). Take the LPC
   envelope (`lpcBurg` of {{P46}}, order 18, pre-emphasis 0.97) of the last
   40 ms, and subtract the correction `C` = the envelope measured in the
   same way for the neutral vowel minus the `tractResponse` of the neutral
   vowel. This removes the source and leaves the tract. Such rows are at
   most 10 % of the table.
6. Rows. All random numbers come from `createRandom` of `src/random.js`
   seeded with `options.seed` (default 1) plus the row number, so a row does
   not depend on the rows before it. The head is `head` with
   `vocalTractLength` set to `L0`.
   1. Seeds: the neutral vowel, the 31 vowels (`vowelTarget` and
      `vowelChannels`), the 31 vowels nasalised, the 66 consonants
      (`consonantTarget`) each on the vowels `a`, `i`, `u` and neutral.
      Stored with the phoneme number in `phone`.
   2. Mixtures: for 25 % of the remaining rows pick two seeds and mix all
      channels linearly with weight 0.25, 0.5 or 0.75.
   3. Perturbed seeds: for 50 %, pick a seed and add to each channel a
      Gaussian number with a standard deviation of 10 % of its range.
   4. Random vowels: for 25 %, draw `height`, `backness` and `rounding`
      uniformly, `rhotic` 0 (1 in one case of ten), no constriction.

   Clip to the ranges of step 1. Drop a row whose features differ from an
   earlier row by less than 0.5 dB RMS over the envelope while all its
   channels differ by less than 2 % of the range.

7. Layout (typed arrays, row-major; `size` rows):
   - `articulation`: `Uint8Array`, 12 per row, each value `v` stored as
     `round(255 * (v - min) / (max - min))`;
   - `formants`: `Int16Array`, 8 per row: F1 to F4 and B1 to B4 in Hz;
   - `envelope`: `Uint8Array`, 24 per row, steps of 0.5 dB, 0 = -64 dB;
   - `key`: `Float32Array`, 6 per row;
   - `phone`: `Uint16Array`, index into `listSymbols()` or `0xffff`;
   - `tree`: `Int32Array`, a k-d tree over `key` (split on the dimension
     with the largest spread, at the median).

   About 80 bytes per row: 1.6 MB for the default `size` of 20000.

8. `buildCodebook(head, options)` returns an object with the fields
   `version`, `id`, `size`, `lengthScale`, `channels`, the six arrays of
   step 7, `stats` and the function `query`. Options: `size` (default
   20000, at most 100000), `seed` (1), `cache` (an object with `get(id)`
   and `set(id, bytes)`; default a `Map` in memory). `id` is `hashSeed` of
   a string made of `version`, `size`, `seed` and the head values
   `pharynxRatio`, `oralWidth`, `nasalSize`, `nasalCoupling`.
   `lengthScale = head.vocalTractLength / L0`. When the cache has `id`, the
   table is parsed and not built. `serializeCodebook(codebook)` gives a
   `Uint8Array` and `parseCodebook(bytes)` reads it back.
9. `frameEnvelope(frames, t, codebook, out)` gives the observed side: for
   each `GRID[i]` compute `lpcLevel` ({{P46}}) of frame `t` at
   `GRID[i] / lengthScale` Hz. Points above `0.95 * rate / 2` are marked
   absent (`NaN`). Then apply steps 4.3 and 4.4; absent points among the
   first 16 take the value of the highest present point.
10. `codebook.query(envelope, k, out)`: walk the tree to the leaf of the
    key, visit at most 512 rows with back-tracking, keep the 64 nearest by
    key, then order these by the mean squared difference of the envelope
    over the present points (dB²). Write the best `k` (default 16) row
    numbers into `out.index` and their distances into `out.cost`. No
    allocation.
11. `stats`: number of rows per kind, build time, and the share of rows that
    have another row with all formants within 3 % and at least one channel
    more than 25 % of its range away. This is the measured non-uniqueness.

Budgets on the reference machine of {{P46}}: build at most 60 s for 20000
rows on one core (about 200 frequencies times 44 sections per analytic row,
and one render of 80 ms per nasal row); the target is 30 s. One `query`
takes at most 50 microseconds, which is 1 % of real time at 200 frames per
second. The app must build the table in a worker ({{P62}}).

## Tests

New file `tests/recognition-codebook.test.js`; unit tests use `size: 2000`.

- `fast response equals tubeTransfer`: 20 shapes, 50 frequencies each: the
  difference is at most 0.01 dB.
- `is deterministic`: two builds give the same bytes; `parseCodebook` of
  `serializeCodebook` gives equal arrays.
- `finds its own rows`: the envelope of each of 200 rows returns that row
  first.
- `tree agrees with a full search`: for 200 random envelopes the best row
  of `query` is the best row of a full search in at least 99 %.
- `covers unseen shapes`: 500 shapes drawn as in step 6 with another seed:
  the best of the 16 returned rows has F1, F2 and F3 within 5 % in at least
  95 % of the shapes (with `size: 20000`, slow test).
- `scales with tract length`: build with length 14 cm directly (set `L0`
  aside) and compare with the scaled default table: formants of the vowel
  seeds agree within 2 %. If this fails, add the length rounded to 0.5 cm
  to `id` and build per length; state that in the pull request.
- `matches rendered sound`: the 31 vowel seeds rendered for 300 ms
  (`waveguide`, 16000 Hz and 8000 Hz): `query` of the middle frame returns
  a row with `height` and `backness` within 0.15 among the 16 in at least
  90 % (target; the measured share is printed).
- `stays within the CPU budget`: prints `codebook build <s>` and
  `codebook query <microseconds>`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `node --test tests/recognition-codebook.test.js` passes on Node.js,
      Bun and Deno.
- [ ] Two builds with the same arguments on two machines of CI give the same
      SHA-256 of `serializeCodebook` (the test prints it).
- [ ] The build of 20000 rows takes at most 60 s and a query at most 50
      microseconds in the CI log.
- [ ] `npm pack --dry-run` lists no codebook file.
- [ ] `stats` is printed by the slow test and the share of non-unique rows is
      written into the pull request.
- [ ] `buildCodebook`, `serializeCodebook`, `parseCodebook`, `frameEnvelope`
      and `CODEBOOK_CHANNELS` are exported and typed.
- [ ] `npm run check` and `npm test` pass; a minor changeset is added.

## Out of scope

- Choosing one row per frame and smoothing over time: {{P53}}.
- Storing the table in IndexedDB in the app: {{P62}}.
- A nasal branch in the analytic transfer function. Rows with an open velum
  use short renders here.
- Building the table in Rust or on several cores: {{P65}}.
